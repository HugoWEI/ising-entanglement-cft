# Phase-resolved modular-capacity revivals: a posteriori source certification and published-theory audit

**Research continuation — 29 October 2026.** Working problem: half-filled spinless fermions on an open chain, global staggered-mass quench, central adjacent onsite defect. The *new contribution in this note* is a targeted, source-only **two-defect-site a posteriori error bound** and a **continuous-time residual majorant** for piecewise-linear defect drives, together with a first implementation of the directly applicable *conformal* published Rényi-revival baseline. This is a **research working note, not a claim of novelty or a publication-ready theorem**. The exact-arithmetic identities are proved below, while the quoted numerical majorants are evaluated in ordinary floating point, *not* interval-enclosed.

## 1. Status before this continuation

The previous work had: (i) exact Gaussian correlation-matrix dynamics; (ii) Bessel-image / two-site Volterra representation of the propagator; (iii) a first-return Airy surrogate with physical polar normalization and small empirical peak errors; (iv) a rigorous omitted-image tail bound; (v) endpoint-safe capacity continuity and conditional peak-location stability estimates. **What remained unfinished** was bounding the Airy remainder and time discretization tightly enough to give a source-only nonvacuous peak-time interval. See the earlier note `phase_revival_certificate_step/RESEARCH_NOTE.md` in the prior research bundle.

The relevant Hamiltonians are

\[
h_0=-\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}(|j\rangle\langle j+1|+|j+1\rangle\langle j|),\quad
h=h_0+BVB^\dagger,\quad V=\operatorname{diag}(u,v),\quad B=(|L/2-1\rangle,|L/2\rangle).
\]

The initial Slater determinant is represented by \(Q\in\mathbb C^{L\times L/2}\), \(Q^\dagger Q=I\), prepared as the lowest half of the eigenstates of \(h_0+m\operatorname{diag}((-1)^j)\). Time is in hopping units \(J=\hbar=1\).

## 2. The new source-only residual theorem

For **any** continuous candidate defect amplitude \(Y:[0,T]\to\mathbb C^{2\times L/2}\), define a full-chain clean-driven approximation

\[
F_Y(t)=U_0(t)Q-i\int_0^t U_0(t-s)BVY(s)\,ds,\qquad U_0(t)=e^{-ih_0t}.
\]

Define the self-consistency residual using only the *two defect rows*:

\[
r_Y(t)=Y(t)-B^\dagger F_Y(t).
\]

**Theorem (exact arithmetic).** Let \(F(t)=e^{-iht}Q\). Then for every \(t\in[0,T]\),

\[
\boxed{\|F(t)-F_Y(t)\|_2\leq b\int_0^t\|r_Y(s)\|_2\,ds,\qquad b=\|V\|_2=\max(|u|,|v|).}
\]

**Proof.** By differentiation of the clean Duhamel equation,
\(\dot F_Y=-ih_0F_Y-iBVY\). Hence its defective Schrödinger residual is
\(\dot F_Y+ihF_Y=-iBV(Y-B^\dagger F_Y)=-iBVr_Y\). Duhamel's formula for \(F-F_Y\), whose initial value is zero, yields
\[
F(t)-F_Y(t)=i\int_0^t e^{-ih(t-s)}BVr_Y(s)\,ds.
\]
The exact propagator is unitary, \(\|B\|_2=1\), and the theorem follows. **No Grönwall exponential appears.** The same argument bounds all initially occupied modes simultaneously in operator norm. □

This is a customized application of standard variation of constants, **not evidence of a new general mathematical theorem**.

### Why this is more useful than the old projector residual

The previous full-projector derivative residual was large and delicate under gauge rotations. Here all numerical inconsistency is concentrated in a \(2\times(L/2)\) residual. A candidate drive \(Y\) can be generated by a Bessel-image, Airy-wave or other reduced scattering method; constructing \(F_Y\) does not require diagonalization of \(h\).

The theorem controls the reconstructed \(F_Y\), **not automatically the original polar-normalized Airy approximation** \(X_{\mathrm{Ai}}\). The further source-only comparison \(\|F_Y-X_{\mathrm{Ai}}\|\) must be included; for unnormalized \(Z_{\mathrm{Ai}}\), \(\|F-Y_{\mathrm{Ai}}\|\leq b\int\|r\|+\|F_Y-Z_{\mathrm{Ai}}\|\), and polar normalization requires its own perturbation bound.

## 3. Exact integration of a piecewise-linear candidate drive

Let nodal data \(Y_k\) be joined linearly over each cell \([t_k,t_{k+1}]\) of width \(h\). Because the *clean* open chain is diagonalized by the explicit discrete sine transform,

\[
S_{jk}=\sqrt{\frac2{L+1}}\sin\frac{(j+1)(k+1)\pi}{L+1},\quad \epsilon_k=-2\cos\frac{(k+1)\pi}{L+1},
\]

all clean convolutions against a linear drive can be evaluated **analytically in time** at the nodes. In a clean energy channel with \(z=-i\epsilon_k h\), write

\[
\phi_1(z)=\frac{e^z-1}{z},\qquad
\phi_2(z)=\frac{e^z-1-z}{z^2}.
\]

For the accumulated convolution \(D_k\) in that energy channel,

\[
\boxed{D_{k+1}=e^zD_k+h\phi_1(z)\widehat Y_k+h\phi_2(z)(\widehat Y_{k+1}-\widehat Y_k)},
\]

where \(\widehat Y_k=S^\dagger BVY_k\). The code uses a convergent power series near \(z=0\) to avoid cancellation. This is **exact quadrature of the interpolated drive**, not exact quadrature of the unknown physical drive. The discrepancy is detected by \(r_Y\).

### Continuous-time Hermite majorant (exact-arithmetic formula)

On each cell \([a,a+h]\), \(r=Y-B^\dagger F_Y\) is four times differentiable in the open interval. Let \(r_a,r_b\) and its one-sided derivatives \(r'_a,r'_b\) be obtained from the clean-forced evolution; the slope of \(Y\) is constant inside the cell. Cubic Hermite interpolation and its Peano remainder give

\[
\boxed{\int_a^{a+h}\|r(t)\|_2dt\leq
\frac h2(\|r_a\|_2+\|r_b\|_2)
+\frac{h^2}{12}(\|r'_a\|_2+\|r'_b\|_2)
+\frac{h^5}{384}M_4,}
\]

where \(M_4\geq\sup_{[a,b]}\|r^{(4)}\|_2\). Importantly, on a linear-drive cell,

\[
F_Y^{(4)}=h_0^4F_Y+h_0^3BVY+2i h_0^2BVY',
\]

and \(r^{(4)}=-B^\dagger F_Y^{(4)}\). With \(b=\|V\|\), \(Y_{\max}=\max(\|Y_a\|,\|Y_b\|)\), and \(s=(Y_b-Y_a)/h\), a fully explicit choice is

\[
M_4=16(\|F_Y(a)\|_2+h bY_{\max})+8bY_{\max}+8b\|s\|_2,
\]

since \(\|h_0\|_2\leq 2\) and the clean forced solution over one cell obeys \(\|F_Y(t)\|_2\leq\|F_Y(a)\|_2+h bY_{\max}\). This majorant controls the **whole interval between time samples**, unlike summing just nodal residuals. Ordinary floating point does not automatically make it a formally certified numerical enclosure.

### Airy approximation versus Bessel image — a distinct error component

At \(L=48\), \(\Delta t=.1\), sampled up to \(T=30\), comparing the leading *uniform* Airy first-wrap kernel directly against its corresponding exact Bessel-image kernel, each applied to the same occupied initial modes \(Q\):

- Maximum \(\|(W_{\rm Ai}-W_{\rm Bessel})Q\|_2\): **4.97017×10⁻⁵**.
- Maximum difference between the two polar-normalized occupied-space projectors: **3.74047×10⁻⁵** for forward defect and **3.51282×10⁻⁵** for reverse.
- Smallest computed singular value of these underlying amplitude matrices over the first-revival region: above **0.999**.
- The known polar perturbation bound on the sampled grid is below **9.95×10⁻⁵** for the projector difference.

These are useful **a posteriori sampled diagnostics**, independent of the final defective Hamiltonian, but **not a validated uniform Airy remainder theorem**. Sampling between nodes and special-function rounding must be bounded before treating them as rigorous numbers.

The earlier analytic omitted-Bessel-image bound *is* exact-arithmetic: for \(L=48\), one image shell and \(T=30\), the clean-kernel bound is 5.1603×10⁻¹² and the continuous-time impurity response bound is 7.0644×10⁻⁹. That controls a different component of the error budget.

## 5. Why the full peak-time certificate remains unfinished

The endpoint-safe continuity estimate for fermionic capacity is

\[
|C_A(P)-C_A(\widetilde P)|\leq \frac L2\,\omega_f(\|P-\widetilde P\|_2),\quad
f(x)=x(1-x)\log^2\frac{1-x}{x},
\]

and a smoothed, isolated peak obeys a **conditional** error estimate \(|\Delta t|\leq \|g'_\sigma\|_1\varepsilon_C/\kappa\) once the current discrepancy, local curvature and uniqueness/global prominence have been certified. The Gaussian convolution's finite-window boundary tails also require control.

At \(h=.025\), the present source-only amplitude error scale \(\sim .01\) maps to a conservative projector uncertainty \(\sim .04\). For \(L=48\), the endpoint-safe capacity modulus then permits capacity errors on the order of **nine capacity units** (using the true scalar modulus at \(\delta\simeq.04\)), comparable with the whole capacity itself. **That is a vacuous peak-location bound.** The sharp numerical peak fits previously reported are therefore *not* certified peak-time intervals. Interval validation of the linear-drive residual will not by itself fix the looseness: a higher-order defect solver and/or spectral-window localization is needed.

A separate caveat: our source-only theorem certifies \(F_Y\), not every earlier polar-normalized wavefront curve automatically. One must include the computable discrepancy \(F_Y-Z_{\rm Ai}\) and polar normalization before aggregating the bounds.

## 6. Published-theory comparison: stronger than a zero-shift null

We inspected Capizzi and Eisler, **“Entanglement evolution after a global quench across a conformal defect,”** *SciPost Physics* 14, 070 (2023), arXiv:2209.03297, particularly §3.4, Eq. (64), and §3.5, Eq. (66). Their Eq. (64) already describes **coherently recombined quasiparticle paths** after defect and boundary reflections. Their results also explicitly distinguish reflection-asymmetric initial staggerings, and §3.5 treats the short-time momentum-dependent *hopping* defect. This rules out claims that phase coherence, finite-size revivals, or initial-state asymmetry are newly discovered here.

Because their result is an expression for Rényi entropies \(S_n\), the *existing* capacity prediction follows immediately via \(C=-2\partial_nS_n|_{n=1}\). `published_baseline.py` implements this derivative of Eq. (64) in the paper's **conformal, scale-invariant model**, with its correct hopping and mass unit conversion. Two analytically required controls pass: zero transmission gives zero quasiparticle capacity contribution, and unity transmission reproduces the homogeneous quasiparticle expression.

The direct numerical comparison in that *applicable homogeneous limit*, \(L=48\), \(m=2.5\), gives capacity-change RMSE **0.8402** across \(t\in[0,38.4]\), with maximum absolute deviation **1.4068**. This shows that differentiating a leading quasiparticle Rényi ansatz does not automatically accurately predict finite-size capacity. It is **not** a head-to-head test of Eq. (64) for our two-site onsite-defect model: Eq. (64)'s assumptions do not cover that model, and reusing it there without extension would be unjustified.

### Boundary of a potential scientific contribution

A plausible next result would be a **momentum-dependent, reflection-phase-resolved, onsite-defect first-revival capacity formula** with a *nonvacuous* explicit error budget and out-of-sample tests, which demonstrably improves over an appropriately extended published coherent-quasiparticle model. The Green function, Airy approximation, two-site Duhamel residual, Hermite interpolation, and polar identities are established techniques. **No claim of novelty is supported yet.**

## 7. Concrete next moves

1. Replace second-order trapezoidal defect-drive samples by a fourth- or sixth-order Volterra collocation / exponential-integrator scheme, keeping the error check independent through the two-site source-residual theorem. Aim to reduce the \(h=.025\) source residual by orders of magnitude before attempting the peak-time certificate.
2. Construct a **validated uniform** Airy error envelope from DLMF §10.20 / Olver's error bounds or a separately verified Bessel-versus-Airy interval computation across the entire first-peak window; the current \(5\times10^{-5}\) grid observation is not sufficient.
3. Introduce validated numerical special functions and outward-rounded interval linear algebra, including Gaussian smoothing's endpoint padding, first-prominent-peak selection, and local uniqueness. These are separate from exact-arithmetic proofs.
4. Implement an actual finite-size *conformal defect* test of the published Eq. (64) and derive a defensible extension for our two-site onsite defect, rather than applying Eq. (64) outside its conditions.
5. Evaluate held-out defect pairs, initial stagger strengths and \(L\) values; archive all negative results.

## Reproduction

From this directory, use a Python environment with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib:

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m unittest -v test_certificate.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python run_benchmarks.py
```

`defect_residual.py` computes the full-chain source-only residual, using the *clean* spectral basis; `airy_error_budget.py` independently compares the explicit first-return Airy and Bessel kernels; `published_baseline.py` implements the valid-domain published Rényi-capacity baseline. The `src/` directory contains the minimal earlier Bessel–Volterra/Airy modules needed to repeat these diagnostics. `diagnostics.json` contains the reported floating-point results.
