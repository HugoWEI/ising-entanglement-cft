# Clean-propagator validation of an Airy-generated Hermite drive

**30 October 2026 · research prototype · six passing regression tests.**

This note gives a **source-only, exact-arithmetic conditional** bound on the occupied-amplitude error of a *hybrid* first-return predictor. The impurity drive `Y` is generated by our fourth-order Airy Volterra solver, but the output state is reconstructed with the **exact clean open-chain propagator** (sine eigenmodes are explicit). The final defective Hamiltonian is **never diagonalized for prediction/certification**. That diagonalization appears in a *separate post-hoc validation script*.

The central advantage is that the error bound does not require the Airy kernel's high derivatives or interval evaluation of Airy special functions. It instead validates the **resulting drive** directly against the known clean propagator.

## 1. State and two-site defect

Take an open hopping chain of length `L` with Hermitian matrix `h0` having entries `-1` on neighboring sites. The final Hamiltonian is `hf=h0+B V B†`, where `B` embeds the two central sites and `V=diag(u,v)`. Initial occupied orbitals form an isometry `Q ∈ C^(L×m)`, `Q†Q=I`, `m=L/2`, with staggered-mass initial Hamiltonian (`m0=2.5`).

For any continuously differentiable, piecewise cubic proposed impurity response `Y(t) ∈ C^(2×m)`, define the **exactly reconstructed clean-witness state**

`F_Y(t)=U0(t)Q-i ∫_0^t U0(t-s) B VY(s) ds`, `U0(t)=exp(-ih0 t)`.

Let `q(t)=B†F_Y(t)-Y(t)`. Direct differentiation shows

`F_Y'(t)+i hf F_Y(t)=i B V q(t)`, and `F_Y(0)=Q`,

and Duhamel with **unitary defective-chain evolution** gives the exact inequality

`||F_exact(t)-F_Y(t)||_2 ≤ b ∫_0^t ||q(s)||_2 ds`, `b=||V||_2`.

All quantities on the right can be computed without a defective-chain solver.

## 2. Entire-cell Hermite remainder

Let `t_j=jh`, `h<1/2` and `Y` be the cubic Hermite function determined by the computed node values `Y_j,Y'_j`. On each interval `I_j=[t_j,t_{j+1}]` let `z(t)=B†F_Y(t)`. Then `q=z-Y`. Since `Y` is cubic Hermite interpolation of its own node values/derivatives, **q's interpolating polynomial** depends solely on `q_j=z_j-Y_j` and `q'_j=z'_j-Y'_j`.

The Peano remainder estimate for cubic Hermite interpolation gives, in operator norm,

`||q(t)-H_j q(t)||_2 ≤ M4_j (t-t_j)^2(t_{j+1}-t)^2/24`,

provided `M4_j ≥ sup_(s∈I_j) ||z''''(s)||_2`. For matrices, apply the scalar Peano estimate after arbitrary norm-one dual linear functionals and take a supremum; no coordinate-dependent factor is necessary. Integrating the absolute Hermite basis functions yields

`∫_(I_j) ||q(t)||_2 dt ≤ h/2(||q_j||_2+||q_{j+1}||_2)+h²/12(||q'_j||_2+||q'_{j+1}||_2)+h^5 M4_j/720`.

The constant `1/720` is sharp for the integrated remainder; `q(t)=(t-t_j)^2(t-t_{j+1})^2` gives equality with `M4=24`.

**Important regularity:** the Hermite drive is globally `C^1` and piecewise cubic; `z` has four classical derivatives *inside* each open interval. No global fourth-derivative assumption across knots is made.

## 3. Explicit, conservative M4_j majorant without Airy derivatives

The witness obeys `F_Y'=-ih0 F_Y-i B VY`. Differentiating this equation *within a cell* and using `||h0||_2≤2` gives

`||z''''||_2 ≤ 16||F_Y||_2+8b||Y||_2+4b||Y'||_2+2b||Y''||_2+b||Y'''||_2`.

Write `A_j=Y_j`, `D_j=Y'_j`, and let `E_0=Y''(t_j+)`, `E_1=Y''(t_{j+1}-)` and `E_3=Y'''|_(I_j)` be the **exact polynomial derivatives** from cubic Hermite formulas. The `Y''` matrix is affine, hence `M2=max(||E_0||_2,||E_1||_2)` is a bound everywhere inside the interval. Set

`M1=||D_j||_2+h M2`, `M0=||A_j||_2+h||D_j||_2+h² M2/2`, `M3=||E_3||_2`.

Use the local ODE and `||h0||≤2`:

`MF_j ≤ (||F_Y(t_j)||_2+h b M0)/(1-2h)`.

Therefore an explicit *a priori within-cell* derivative majorant is

`M4_j=16 MF_j+8b M0+4b M1+2b M2+b M3`.

These are **algebraic bounds given exact nodal inputs**. Numerical estimates shown below use `numpy.linalg.norm`, clean sine-mode propagation, and 12-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature. They are not outward-rounded interval enclosures of the inputs.

## 4. Independent numerical verification

At `L=48`, `T=30`, step `h=0.05`, 12 Gauss nodes per clean-convolution cell:

| Pair `(u,v)` | Conditional amplitude upper (full interval) | Measured max amplitude error (post-hoc exact benchmark) | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| (1.20,0.35) | 1.4926346e-5 | 3.2369845e-6 | 4.61 |
| (0.35,1.20) | 1.2767532e-5 | 2.0890165e-6 | 6.11 |

At `h=0.10` the corresponding conditional upper bounds are `2.6297126e-4` and `2.2597939e-4`; the observed maxima are `5.1700999e-5`, `3.3365649e-5`. Numerical convergence is consistent with fourth-order interpolation error.

The 8- versus 12-point clean-convolution difference at the nodes was below `3e-16` in the tested runs. **This is a quadrature convergence check, not a rigorous quadrature error proof.**

### Predictor first-revival times

Using `T=36`, `h=0.05`, Gaussian sample smoother width `sigma=2`:

| Pair | Pure Airy Hermite | Airy drive + exact clean reconstruction | Exact defective Hamiltonian (post-hoc only) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| (1.20,0.35) | 26.42266895 | 26.42267701 | 26.42267971 |
| (0.35,1.20) | 27.31179029 | 27.31193236 | 27.31194477 |
| (0.50,0.20), held out | 26.47975668 | 26.47976145 | 26.47976117 |

This hybrid predictor is more accurate in these three tests but is **not a reduced Airy-only end-to-end propagation**: it uses the full clean-chain propagator in output reconstruction. It **does not fit** any capacity peak to the defective-Hamiltonian benchmark.

### Conditional peak-current transfer diagnostic

A source-only amplitude error bound `e_j` at each sample node implies, by the previously derived physical Gaussian-state geometry bound (with `M<1.22`),

`|C_exact(t_j)-C(polar F_Y(t_j))| ≤ 2M m e_j/(1-e_j)` (assuming `e_j<1`).

For normalized Gaussian weights `w_j(x)` of width `sigma=2`,

`|C̄_exact'(x)-C̄_witness'(x)| ≤ Σ_j |w'_j(x)| eps_Cj`,

and an analogous second-derivative inequality holds. The script `conditional_peak.py` evaluates current and curvature envelope *on a dense mesh*. The resulting local error **diagnostics** at `T=36,h=.05` are approximately `±0.0172`, `±0.0415`, and `±0.00104` for the three pairs. These are **not certified brackets**: extrema between mesh points, floating-point uncertainties, and the verification of the globally first qualifying peak remain outstanding.

## 5. What remains

1. Outward-rounded or arbitrary-precision enclosure of the clean sine-mode matrix functions and *all* Gauss–Legendre convolution integrals.
2. Validated norms and singular values at each Hermite node, followed by outward-rounded arithmetic in the algebraic inequalities.
3. Continuous-in-peak-time interval bounds for the Gaussian current and curvature; independent check of first-qualifying prominence.
4. Out-of-sample larger systems and stronger defects; comparison with existing coherent-revival work (Capizzi–Eisler, *SciPost Phys.* **14**, 070 (2023), DOI 10.21468/SciPostPhys.14.4.070).

The Hermite remainder, the unitary Duhamel estimate and pure Gaussian-state Lipschitz continuity are established mathematical methods. This is a model-specific integration of those methods and a reproducible conditional numerical result—not yet a novel physics theorem or publication-grade formal certification.
